# Granola meeting notes and Google Calendar

## What this source holds

- Granola: notes and transcripts from calls with suppliers, freelancers, advisers, and platform reps
- Google Calendar: when those calls happened, who attended, and the event description or agenda

Calls are where many small-business decisions get made and never written down anywhere else.

## How to search it

Granola tool names vary by version. Read the server's tool list first. The server may need authentication. If it fails, report the gap and continue with Calendar only.

1. **Find the meetings in Calendar.** Use `search_events` with the vendor, freelancer, or topic name, or `list_events` over the decision's date range. Note titles, dates, and attendees.
2. **Find matching notes in Granola.** Search by meeting title, attendee, or topic, and by the dates from Calendar.
3. **Read the full note**, and the transcript if one exists. Look for the moment a choice was made and the reason given.
4. **Use Calendar alone for dating.** Even without notes, a call with a supplier two days before a price change is indirect evidence. Record it as such.

You are read-only. Do not create, update, delete, or respond to events.

## What good evidence looks like

- A note line such as "Agreed to switch to weekly dispatch, they can't do daily pickups below 20 parcels"
- An action item that matches the change in code or in the store
- A transcript passage where the reason is stated in the speaker's own words

## Common pitfalls

- **AI summaries are not quotes.** Granola's summary is a generated reading of the call. Prefer the transcript wording when it exists, and label summary text as summary.
- **Planned is not decided.** An agenda item shows a topic, not an outcome.
- **Missing notes.** Many calls have no notes. A calendar event with no note is a gap. Name it.

## What to return

For each relevant meeting: title, date, attendees by role, the lines that bear on the question (marked as transcript or summary), and the note or event ID.
