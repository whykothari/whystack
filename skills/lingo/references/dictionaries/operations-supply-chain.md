# Operations and supply chain

The work of planning demand, buying, making, storing, and moving goods, and of running and improving the processes behind them. Planners, buyers, plant and warehouse managers, logistics coordinators, quality engineers, and operations leads use these terms.

## Terms

### Planning and forecasting

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| S&OP (sales and operations planning) | Monthly process that matches the demand plan to supply | You align sales, supply, and finance on one plan | core |
| IBP (integrated business planning) | S&OP extended to finance and strategy over a longer horizon | You tie the operating plan to budget and strategy | expert |
| Demand planning | Making and managing the forecast of what customers will order | You build or own the forecast | core |
| Consensus forecast | One forecast that sales, marketing, and operations agree on | You close the monthly demand review | core |
| MAPE (mean absolute percentage error) | Average forecast miss, as a percent of actual demand | You report forecast accuracy | core |
| Forecast bias | A steady lean toward over- or under-forecasting | You check whether misses lean one way | core |
| Aggregate planning | Mid-term plan for total output, labor, and inventory | You plan capacity by product family, not by item | expert |
| MPS (master production schedule) | Plan of which finished items to build in which period | You turn the S&OP plan into a build plan | core |
| MRP (material requirements planning) | Calculates which parts to buy or build, and when | You plan components from the MPS and BOM | core |
| ATP (available to promise) | Stock or planned supply not yet committed to orders | You quote a delivery date to a customer | expert |
| Bullwhip effect | Order swings that grow larger at each step upstream | You explain upstream volatility or excess stock | core |

### Procurement and sourcing

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| RFQ (request for quotation) | Formal ask for prices on a defined item | The spec is fixed and price decides | core |
| RFP (request for proposal) | Formal ask for solutions and prices on a need | Suppliers must propose how to meet the need | core |
| PO (purchase order) | Buyer's binding order to a supplier | You commit to buy | core |
| MOQ (minimum order quantity) | Smallest order a supplier will accept | You size orders or negotiate terms | core |
| Landed cost | Full unit cost at your door, with freight and duty | You compare suppliers or set prices | core |
| TCO (total cost of ownership) | All costs of a buy over its whole life | You compare offers beyond unit price | core |
| Supplier scorecard | Regular rating of a supplier on set metrics | You review supplier performance | core |
| Spend analysis | Sorting and studying what the company buys | You find savings or pick sourcing targets | core |
| Category management | Managing related spend as one group with one strategy | You set a sourcing plan by spend group | expert |
| Should-cost | Estimate of what an item ought to cost to make | You challenge a supplier price | expert |
| Single vs dual sourcing | One supplier for an item, or two | You trade price against supply risk | core |

### Manufacturing and production

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| BOM (bill of materials) | Structured list of parts and amounts in a product | You cost, plan, or build a product | core |
| Lead time | Time from order or start until the item is ready | You plan when to order or start | core |
| Cycle time | Time one unit takes through a step or process | You measure process speed | core |
| Takt time | Available time divided by demand; the needed pace | You set line pace to match demand | core |
| Design vs effective capacity | Maximum output, versus output under real conditions | You plan load or judge a capacity gap | core |
| Utilization | Share of available capacity actually used | You check how loaded a resource is | core |
| Bottleneck | The step that limits total process output | You decide where to add capacity | core |
| WIP (work in process) | Items started but not finished | You track flow or tied-up cash | core |
| Yield | Share of good output from total input | You track losses in a process | core |
| FPY (first pass yield) | Share of units good the first time, no rework | You measure process quality at a step | expert |
| OEE (overall equipment effectiveness) | Availability times performance times quality for a machine | You measure equipment productivity | core |
| MTS / MTO / ETO (make to stock, order, engineer) | Build before orders, after orders, or to custom design | You pick a production and inventory strategy | core |

### Inventory management

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| SKU (stock keeping unit) | Code for one distinct item you stock | You count, plan, or report inventory | core |
| Safety stock | Extra stock held against demand and supply surprises | You set buffers for variability | core |
| Reorder point (ROP) | Stock level that triggers a new order | You set when to replenish | core |
| EOQ (economic order quantity) | Order size that minimizes ordering plus holding cost | You set how much to order | core |
| ABC analysis | Ranking items by value to set control effort | You decide which items get tight control | core |
| Cycle counting | Counting a few items often instead of yearly | You keep stock records accurate | core |
| Inventory turnover | COGS divided by average inventory | You judge how fast stock moves | core |
| DOH (days of inventory on hand) | How many days current stock will last | You check stock coverage | core |
| Service level | Target chance of not running out in a cycle | You set safety stock policy | expert |
| Fill rate | Share of demand shipped from stock right away | You measure stock availability | core |
| Carrying cost | Yearly cost to hold stock, as share of value | You weigh stock levels against cost | core |
| VMI (vendor managed inventory) | Supplier watches and refills the buyer's stock | You shift replenishment to the supplier | expert |
| E&O (excess and obsolete inventory) | Stock above need or no longer sellable | You plan write-downs or clear-outs | expert |

### Warehousing, logistics, and trade

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3PL (third-party logistics) | Outside firm that stores, packs, or ships for you | You outsource warehousing or fulfillment | core |
| WMS (warehouse management system) | Software that runs warehouse stock and tasks | You set up or change warehouse operations | core |
| TMS (transportation management system) | Software that plans, books, and tracks freight | You manage carriers and shipments | core |
| Cross-docking | Moving inbound goods straight to outbound, no storage | You cut storage time in distribution | core |
| Pick and pack | Pulling order items from shelves and boxing them | You design or cost order fulfillment | core |
| FTL / LTL (full truckload, less than truckload) | Whole truck for one shipper, or shared truck | You choose a road freight mode | core |
| FCL / LCL (full container load, less than container load) | Whole ocean container, or shared container space | You choose an ocean freight mode | core |
| Freight forwarder | Firm that arranges transport for shippers | You ship across borders or modes | core |
| Customs broker | Licensed agent who clears goods through customs | You import goods | core |
| BOL (bill of lading) | Carrier's receipt, contract of carriage, and title document | You ship, claim, or release cargo | core |
| Incoterms 2020 | ICC rules for who pays and bears risk in trade | You write a sales or purchase contract | core |
| EXW / FOB / DDP (ex works, free on board, delivered duty paid) | Buyer does most, risk passes at ship, seller does all | You name the Incoterms rule in a quote | core |
| HS code (Harmonized System code) | Global product code that sets import duty | You classify goods for customs | expert |
| Demurrage and detention | Fees for keeping containers past free time | You plan port pickups and returns | expert |
| Last mile | Final delivery step to the end customer | You plan delivery cost or speed | core |

### Process improvement and quality

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lean | Method to deliver value by removing waste | You improve flow and cut cost | core |
| Muda (waste) | Any step that adds cost but no customer value | You classify waste in a process | core |
| VSM (value stream mapping) | Map of material and information flow end to end | You find waste and delay across a process | core |
| Kaizen | Small, continuous improvements, often in focused events | You run an improvement event | core |
| 5S (sort, set in order, shine, standardize, sustain) | Method to organize and keep a workplace clean | You set up or audit a work area | core |
| Kanban | Signal card or bin that triggers replenishment | You run a pull system | core |
| Pull system | Make or move only what the next step uses | You replace forecast-pushed production | core |
| Poka-yoke | Mistake-proofing so an error cannot happen | You prevent repeat defects | expert |
| Six Sigma | Data-driven method to cut process variation | You attack defects with statistics | core |
| DMAIC (define, measure, analyze, improve, control) | Five-phase Six Sigma project cycle | You structure an improvement project | core |
| PDCA (plan, do, check, act) | Four-step cycle to test and keep changes | You run a fast improvement loop | core |
| RCA (root cause analysis) | Finding the underlying cause, not the symptom | You investigate a defect or failure | core |
| Fishbone diagram (Ishikawa) | Chart of possible causes sorted by category | You brainstorm causes with a team | core |
| Pareto chart | Bar chart that ranks causes by frequency or cost | You pick the few causes that matter most | core |
| SPC (statistical process control) | Using control charts to watch process variation | You monitor a process for drift | expert |
| Cpk (process capability index) | How well a process fits inside spec limits | You judge if a process can meet spec | expert |
| CAPA (corrective and preventive action) | Formal fix for a problem cause and its recurrence | You close a nonconformance or audit finding | core |

### Project and program management

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project charter | Document that authorizes a project and names its goal | You start a project | core |
| Scope | All work and outputs a project must deliver | You define what is in and out | core |
| WBS (work breakdown structure) | Project work split into smaller deliverable pieces | You plan and estimate a project | core |
| Critical path | Longest chain of tasks; sets the shortest finish | You find tasks that cannot slip | core |
| Float (slack) | Time a task can slip without delaying the project | You decide which tasks can wait | expert |
| Milestone | Key point or event with zero duration | You mark progress checkpoints | core |
| RACI (responsible, accountable, consulted, informed) | Matrix of who does, owns, advises, and hears | You assign roles across a team | core |
| Baseline | Approved scope, schedule, and cost to measure against | You track variance | expert |
| Change control | Formal process to approve scope or plan changes | You stop unapproved scope growth | core |
| Risk register | Log of risks with owner, impact, and response | You manage project risk | core |

### Business operations and metrics

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| SOP (standard operating procedure) | Written step-by-step instructions for a routine task | You document how a task is done | core |
| KPI (key performance indicator) | Main metric that shows if a goal is met | You pick what to measure | core |
| OKR (objectives and key results) | Goal plus a few measurable results that prove it | You set quarterly team goals | core |
| SLA (service level agreement) | Agreed performance standard between provider and client | You contract a 3PL, carrier, or team | core |
| OTIF (on time in full) | Share of orders delivered on the date, complete | You measure delivery to customers | core |
| Perfect order rate | Share of orders on time, complete, undamaged, correctly documented | You report end-to-end order quality | expert |
| Order cycle time | Time from customer order to delivery | You measure responsiveness | core |
| Cash-to-cash cycle time | Days between paying suppliers and collecting from customers | You link operations to working capital | expert |
| COGS (cost of goods sold) | Direct cost of the goods a company sold | You compute margins or turns | core |
| SCOR model (Supply Chain Operations Reference) | ASCM standard map of supply chain processes and metrics | You benchmark or design supply chain processes | expert |

## Phrasebook

| Generic words | Trade words |
|---|---|
| figure out how much we will sell | build a demand forecast; run demand planning |
| get sales and operations on the same page | run the monthly S&OP cycle to a consensus plan |
| how good is our forecast | forecast accuracy (MAPE) and forecast bias |
| we keep running out | low fill rate; review safety stock and reorder point |
| we have too much stuff sitting around | excess and obsolete (E&O) inventory; low inventory turns |
| when should we order more | reorder point (ROP) |
| how much should we order | order quantity; EOQ, subject to MOQ |
| a cushion of extra stock | safety stock at a target service level |
| figure out which products matter most | ABC analysis |
| ask suppliers for prices | issue an RFQ |
| the real cost including shipping | landed cost |
| the cheapest supplier | lowest total cost of ownership (TCO), not lowest unit price |
| rate our suppliers | supplier scorecard (OTIF, quality, lead time, cost) |
| list of parts in the product | bill of materials (BOM) |
| how long until it arrives | lead time |
| the slow step holding everything up | bottleneck (constraint) |
| how fast we need to make each one | takt time |
| how much good product comes out | yield; first pass yield (FPY) |
| machines are not running well | low OEE (overall equipment effectiveness) |
| outsource our shipping and storage | use a 3PL |
| who pays for shipping and insurance | the Incoterms 2020 rule, for example FOB or DDP |
| delivery to the customer's door | last-mile delivery |
| orders arrive late or incomplete | low OTIF; low perfect order rate |
| make the process less wasteful | apply lean; map the value stream; remove muda |
| find out why it keeps breaking | root cause analysis (5 whys, fishbone) and CAPA |
| written instructions for the job | standard operating procedure (SOP) |
| who does what on the project | RACI matrix |
| the tasks that cannot be late | critical path |
| the project keeps growing | scope creep; send it through change control |

## Deliverable shapes

| Deliverable | Standard parts |
|---|---|
| S&OP pack | prior-month performance (MAPE, bias, OTIF), demand review, supply and capacity review, gaps and scenarios, financial reconciliation, decisions for the executive meeting |
| Demand forecast | statistical baseline, overrides with reasons, consensus forecast by SKU or family and period, forecast horizon, accuracy and bias by segment, assumptions and risks |
| Inventory policy | ABC segmentation, service level by segment, safety stock method, reorder point and order quantity rules, review period, owner |
| RFQ package | scope and specifications, quantities and forecast, MOQ and lead time questions, Incoterms rule and delivery place, quality and compliance needs, response template, evaluation criteria, deadline |
| Supplier scorecard | supplier, period, OTIF, quality (defect rate or PPM), lead time adherence, cost vs agreed price, responsiveness, weighted score, trend, actions |
| Bill of materials | parent item, component part numbers, descriptions, quantity per, unit of measure, levels, revision, scrap allowance |
| Standard operating procedure (SOP) | purpose, scope, roles, definitions, materials and tools, numbered steps, safety and quality checks, records, revision history and owner |
| DMAIC project charter | problem statement, business case, goal in a metric, scope (in and out), baseline data, team and roles, timeline by phase |
| Value stream map | customer demand and takt time, process steps with cycle time and changeover, inventory between steps, information flow, lead time vs value-added time, future-state map |
| Project plan | charter summary, scope and WBS, schedule with critical path and milestones, RACI, budget baseline, risk register, change control process |
| Freight quote comparison | lanes, mode (FTL, LTL, FCL, LCL, air), transit time, rate and surcharges, Incoterms rule, landed cost per unit, carrier or forwarder |

## Misused or dated terms

| Term | Problem | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| SOP for planning | SOP means standard operating procedure; it is easy to confuse with S&OP | S&OP for sales and operations planning; SOP only for procedures |
| Lead time and cycle time as one thing | Lead time is the full wait to get an item; cycle time is time per unit at a step | Name which one, and its start and end points |
| Forecast accuracy with no metric | The number means nothing without its formula and level | MAPE or weighted MAPE at a stated level, plus bias |
| Just in time means zero inventory | JIT keeps small, steady buffers paced by demand | JIT or pull system with sized kanban buffers |
| Inventory turnover on sales | Sales-based turns mix price with cost and overstate turns | COGS divided by average inventory |
| FOB for any shipment | FOB is an Incoterms rule for sea and inland waterway; US domestic FOB terms differ | FCA for container or multimodal freight; name the rule set |
| DAT (delivered at terminal) | Replaced in Incoterms 2020 | DPU (delivered at place unloaded) |
| APICS Dictionary | Renamed by ASCM | ASCM Supply Chain Dictionary |
| SCOR Make and Deliver | SCOR DS renamed Make to Transform and split Deliver into Order and Fulfill | SCOR DS processes: Orchestrate, Plan, Order, Source, Transform, Fulfill, Return |
| Six Sigma for any improvement | Six Sigma is a specific statistical method with defined roles | Name the method: kaizen, PDCA, or DMAIC |
