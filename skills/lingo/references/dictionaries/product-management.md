# Product management

The work of deciding what a product team builds, why, and for whom, then getting it shipped and adopted. Product managers, product leaders, founders, and product marketers use these terms with design, engineering, and go-to-market teams.

## Terms

### Discovery and research

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product discovery | Work to find out what is worth building | You check ideas before engineering commits to them | core |
| Continuous discovery | Small weekly customer contact by the team that builds | You set up a research habit, not a one-off study | expert |
| Product trio | PM, designer, and tech lead who run discovery together | You assign who owns discovery | expert |
| JTBD (jobs to be done) | Progress a customer wants to make in a given situation | You frame a need without naming a feature | core |
| Problem statement | Who has which problem, the evidence, and why it matters | You open a spec, brief, or discovery plan | core |
| Customer interview | One-to-one talk about past behavior and needs | You plan qualitative research with users or buyers | core |
| Opportunity solution tree (OST) | Map from a target outcome to needs, then solution ideas | You choose which customer need to pursue next | expert |
| Assumption testing | Fast, cheap checks of the riskiest beliefs behind an idea | You want to cut risk before you build | expert |
| Four big risks (value, usability, feasibility, viability) | The four ways a product idea can fail | You review whether an idea is ready to build | expert |
| Hypothesis | A testable belief with an expected, measurable result | You frame a feature bet or experiment | core |
| MVP (minimum viable product) | Smallest release that tests the key hypothesis | You scope a first release to learn, not to finish | core |
| Fake door test | Offer a feature before it exists to measure demand | You test demand before you build | expert |
| Buyer persona | Profile of the person who decides to pay | You shape sales messaging or pricing | core |
| User persona | Profile of the person who uses the product | You shape flows and features | core |
| Win/loss analysis | Study of why deals were won or lost | You look for product or positioning gaps | expert |
| PMF (product-market fit) | A defined market strongly wants and keeps using the product | You decide whether to invest in scaling growth | core |
| Sean Ellis test | Survey: share of users "very disappointed" to lose the product | You measure PMF with a survey (40% is the usual bar) | expert |

### Strategy and positioning

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product vision | Long-range picture of the future the product creates | You align teams on a direction for years | core |
| Product strategy | Choice of which problems to solve to reach the vision | You set focus for the next year or more | core |
| Product operating model | Teams own problems and outcomes, not a feature list | You set up how product, design, and engineering work | expert |
| Positioning | How the product is framed against alternatives for target buyers | You set messaging, category, and target segment | core |
| Competitive alternatives | What customers would do if your product did not exist | You start positioning work, including the status quo | expert |
| Value proposition | The benefit buyers get that alternatives do not give | You write messaging, a landing page, or a pitch | core |
| Market category | Frame of reference that tells buyers what the product is | You name the space you compete in | expert |
| ICP (ideal customer profile) | Type of account that gets the most value fastest | You focus sales, marketing, and the roadmap | core |
| TAM, SAM, SOM (total, serviceable, obtainable market) | Market size at three levels of reach | You size an opportunity or write a business case | core |
| Competitive landscape | The set of rivals and alternatives compared on key factors | You inform strategy, positioning, or sales battlecards | core |
| PLG (product-led growth) | Model where the product itself drives signups and expansion | You design self-serve trial, upgrade, and invite flows | core |

### Prioritization and roadmaps

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product roadmap | Plan of what the team will work on over time | You tell stakeholders the direction and sequence | core |
| Now-Next-Later roadmap | Roadmap grouped by time horizon instead of dates | You avoid false precision on dates | expert |
| Outcome-based roadmap | Roadmap of goals to move, not features to ship | You tie planned work to results | expert |
| Outcomes over output | Judge work by its effect, not by features shipped | You set team goals or review progress | core |
| OKR (objectives and key results) | A goal plus measurable results that show it is met | You set quarterly team goals | core |
| RICE score (reach, impact, confidence, effort) | Reach times impact times confidence, divided by effort | You rank a backlog with a number | core |
| Kano model | Sorts features into basic, performance, and delighter types | You decide what a release must include | expert |
| MoSCoW (must, should, could, won't) | Four labels that split scope by need | You negotiate release scope with stakeholders | core |
| Cost of delay | Value lost for each week the work waits | You sequence work by urgency | expert |
| Product backlog | Ordered list of work for the team | You plan sprints or the next cycle | core |
| Backlog refinement | Session to clarify, size, and order backlog items | You prepare work before sprint planning | core |
| Appetite | Fixed time you agree to spend on a problem | You shape fixed-time, variable-scope work | expert |
| Technical debt | Future cost created by shortcuts in the code | You balance new features against fixes | core |

### Specs and delivery

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| PRD (product requirements document) | Doc that states the problem, goals, and requirements | You hand a feature to design and engineering | core |
| One-pager | Short doc: problem, solution, metrics, scope, open questions | You pitch an idea for approval | core |
| User story | A need written as who, what, and why | You write backlog items | core |
| Acceptance criteria | Conditions a story must meet to count as done | You make scope testable | core |
| Epic | Large body of work split into stories | You group related stories under one goal | core |
| DoD (definition of done) | Team-wide checklist that all finished work must pass | You agree on a quality bar | core |
| Non-goals | What the work will deliberately not cover | You stop scope creep in a spec | expert |
| Edge case | Rare input or state outside the normal flow | You make a spec complete | core |
| Dual-track agile | Discovery and delivery run side by side | You set the team's working rhythm | expert |
| Sprint | Fixed time box of work, often two weeks | You plan delivery cadence | core |
| Feature flag | Switch that turns a feature on or off without a deploy | You stage a rollout or run a test | core |
| Tracking plan | List of events and properties to log for a feature | You make a feature measurable before launch | expert |

### Metrics and growth

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| North Star metric | One metric that best shows value delivered to customers | You align the company on one outcome | core |
| Input metrics | Levers a team can move that drive the North Star | You give each team a goal it controls | expert |
| Leading vs. lagging indicator | Early signal versus after-the-fact result | You pick metrics you can act on in time | core |
| Activation | A new user reaches first real value | You improve onboarding or the first session | core |
| Aha moment | Point where a user first feels the core value | You design onboarding around one key action | core |
| TTV (time to value) | Time from signup to first real value | You speed up onboarding or setup | core |
| Retention rate | Share of users still active after a set period | You judge product health | core |
| Cohort retention curve | Retention of one signup group plotted over time | You check PMF (a flat curve is the signal) | core |
| Churn rate | Share of customers or revenue lost in a period | You track and reduce losses | core |
| NRR (net revenue retention) | Revenue kept from existing customers, with expansion | You judge the quality of SaaS growth | core |
| DAU, WAU, MAU (daily, weekly, monthly active users) | Unique users active in a day, week, or month | You measure usage over time | core |
| Stickiness (DAU/MAU) | Share of monthly users active on a typical day | You judge how habitual use is | expert |
| Feature adoption rate | Share of eligible users who use a feature | You judge whether a launch worked | core |
| AARRR (acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue) | Five-stage growth funnel, also called pirate metrics | You audit a growth funnel stage by stage | core |
| Conversion funnel | Steps toward a goal, with drop-off at each step | You find where users quit | core |
| Growth loop | Cycle where user actions bring in more users | You design growth that compounds | expert |
| A/B test | Randomized test of two versions of one thing | You prove a change caused a result | core |
| Statistical significance | Confidence that a result is not chance | You decide when to call an experiment | core |
| Guardrail metric | Metric that must not get worse during a test | You protect revenue or experience while testing | expert |
| PQL (product qualified lead) | User whose product usage signals intent to buy | You route self-serve users to sales | expert |
| LTV (customer lifetime value) | Total revenue or margin from one customer | You set how much to spend to acquire users | core |
| CAC (customer acquisition cost) | Spend to win one new customer | You judge growth channels or budgets | core |
| NPS (net promoter score) | Score from how likely users are to recommend you | You track customer sentiment over time | core |

### Pricing and packaging

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value metric | The unit you charge for, which grows with customer value | You design a pricing model | core |
| Packaging | How features and limits are grouped into plans | You design or change plans | core |
| Good-better-best | Three plans at rising price and value | You set up a tier structure | core |
| Per-seat pricing | Price set per user | You price tools that grow with team size | core |
| Usage-based pricing | Price set by how much the customer consumes | You price APIs, infrastructure, or AI features | core |
| Freemium | A free plan with paid upgrades | You design a self-serve funnel | core |
| Free trial | Full access for a limited time | You let buyers test before paying | core |
| Feature gating | Holding some features back for higher plans | You decide what goes in each tier | expert |
| WTP (willingness to pay) | Most a customer would pay for a product | You research or set price points | core |
| Van Westendorp price sensitivity meter | Survey of four price thresholds to find an acceptable range | You research a price range | expert |
| Expansion revenue | Extra revenue from upsells, add-ons, and more seats | You plan growth from existing customers | expert |
| ARPU (average revenue per user) | Revenue divided by users or accounts | You track monetization over time | core |
| MRR and ARR (monthly and annual recurring revenue) | Subscription revenue normalized per month or year | You report SaaS revenue | core |

### Launches

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| GTM (go-to-market) plan | Plan for how a product reaches and wins buyers | You prepare a launch or enter a new market | core |
| Launch tier | Size class of a launch that sets effort and channels | You decide how big a launch should be | expert |
| Beta | Early release to a limited group for feedback | You test with real users before full release | core |
| GA (general availability) | Release to all customers | You set the official launch date | core |
| Staged rollout | Release to a growing share of users | You limit risk on a large change | core |
| Go/no-go decision | Final check that launch criteria are met | You confirm a launch is ready | core |
| Sales enablement | Training and tools that help sales sell the product | You prepare sales and support for a launch | core |
| Messaging framework | Approved key messages and proof points per audience | You keep launch copy consistent | expert |
| Release notes | Customer-facing list of what changed in a release | You announce changes to users | core |
| Deprecation (sunset) | Planned removal of a feature or product | You retire something customers use | expert |

## Phrasebook

| Generic words | Trade words |
|---|---|
| figure out what to build | run product discovery |
| talk to some users | run customer interviews (continuous discovery) |
| what users are trying to do | the job to be done (JTBD) |
| check if the idea is any good | test the riskiest assumptions (value, usability, feasibility, viability) |
| a basic first version | an MVP scoped to test one hypothesis |
| see if people want it before building | run a fake door test |
| people really want our product | we have product-market fit (flat cohort retention curve) |
| who we are for and why we are different | positioning: competitive alternatives, unique value, ICP, market category |
| how big the market is | TAM, SAM, SOM |
| decide what to do first | prioritize the backlog (RICE or cost of delay) |
| a plan for the next few months | a Now-Next-Later roadmap tied to outcomes |
| our goals for the quarter | quarterly OKRs |
| write up the feature for engineers | write a PRD with user stories and acceptance criteria |
| things we are not doing | non-goals |
| the one number that matters | North Star metric and its input metrics |
| get new users to the good part faster | improve activation and time to value |
| people stop using it | churn; poor retention in later cohorts |
| keep customers paying more over time | grow net revenue retention through expansion revenue |
| see which version works better | run an A/B test with a primary and a guardrail metric |
| how many people use it regularly | DAU, WAU, MAU and stickiness (DAU/MAU) |
| where people drop off | funnel drop-off by step |
| how we charge | pricing model and value metric |
| the plans we offer | packaging into good-better-best tiers |
| how much people would pay | willingness to pay (Van Westendorp survey) |
| tell everyone it is out | GTM plan, launch tier, release notes, sales enablement |
| roll it out slowly | staged rollout behind a feature flag |
| turn the old feature off | deprecate (sunset) the feature with a migration path |

## Deliverable shapes

| Deliverable | Standard parts |
|---|---|
| PRD (product requirements document) | problem statement, target users, goals and success metrics, non-goals, user stories with acceptance criteria, flows and designs, edge cases, tracking plan, dependencies, risks, launch plan, open questions |
| One-pager | problem, who has it and evidence, proposed solution, success metrics, scope, timeline, open questions |
| Product strategy | vision, target market and ICP, key problems to solve, differentiation, bets and trade-offs, metrics, risks |
| Product roadmap | themes or outcomes, horizons (now, next, later), confidence level, owners, links to OKRs, what is out of scope |
| Opportunity solution tree | target outcome, opportunities (customer needs and pains), solution ideas per opportunity, assumption tests |
| Discovery synthesis | research goal, method and participants, key findings with evidence, jobs and pains, opportunities, recommendations, open questions |
| Experiment brief | hypothesis, primary metric, guardrail metrics, variants, audience and sample size, test length, decision rule, results |
| Positioning statement | competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, best-fit customers, market category, proof points |
| Metrics tree (North Star framework) | North Star metric, input metrics, owning teams, data sources, current baseline, targets |
| Pricing and packaging proposal | value metric, tiers and price points, feature gating per tier, WTP evidence, competitor comparison, revenue impact, migration plan for current customers |
| GTM (go-to-market) launch plan | launch tier, goals and metrics, target segment, positioning and messaging, channels, enablement, timeline to GA, go/no-go criteria, owners |
| Release notes | version and date, new features, improvements, fixes, breaking changes or deprecations, links to docs |

## Misused or dated terms

| Term | Problem | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| MVP (used for a full first version) | An MVP tests a hypothesis; it is not a cut-down v1 | MVP with the hypothesis named, or "v1 scope" |
| Roadmap (used as a date promise) | Dated feature lists set false commitments | Outcome-based or Now-Next-Later roadmap |
| Requirements gathering | Implies users already know the solution | Product discovery |
| Growth hacking | Dated, vague, tactic-focused label | Growth experiments tied to a funnel stage or loop |
| Engagement (with no definition) | Means nothing until you name the action and period | A named metric, for example weekly active users who do the key action |
| Total signups or page views as success | Vanity metrics that do not show value | Activation rate, retention, or the North Star metric |
| Product owner (used for product manager) | A Scrum role for the backlog, not the full PM role | Product manager, unless the team runs Scrum |
| Product-market fit (as a one-time event) | Fit can weaken as markets change | Track PMF signals over time (retention curves, Sean Ellis test) |
