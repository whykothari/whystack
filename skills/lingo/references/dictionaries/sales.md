# Sales

The work of finding buyers, qualifying them, running deals to signature, and forecasting revenue, in B2B and B2C. Sales reps, sales leaders, sales ops, partner managers, and founders who sell use these terms.

## Terms

### Roles and selling motions

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| SDR / BDR (sales or business development representative) | Rep who books first meetings for closers | You split prospecting from closing work | core |
| AE (account executive) | Rep who runs deals from discovery to signature | You name who owns and closes a deal | core |
| SE (sales engineer) | Technical expert who runs demos and proves fit | A deal needs technical validation | expert |
| Full-cycle sales | One rep prospects, runs, and closes each deal | You design a role with no SDR hand-off | expert |
| Inbound sales | Selling to buyers who contacted you first | Leads come from marketing or referrals | core |
| Outbound sales | Selling by contacting buyers who did not ask | You must create pipeline yourself | core |
| Account-based selling (ABS) | Coordinated selling to a named list of target accounts | You focus on few high-value accounts | expert |
| Land and expand | Win a small first deal, then grow the account | You plan entry into a large account | expert |
| Consultative selling | Diagnose the buyer's problem before you propose a fit | Deals are complex and need-driven | core |
| Challenger selling | Teach the buyer a new view of their problem | Buyers think they already know the answer | expert |
| Value selling | Sell on measured business results, not features | You must justify price with ROI | expert |

### Prospecting and outbound

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICP (ideal customer profile) | Traits of the companies that buy and stay | You choose which accounts to target | core |
| Buyer persona | Profile of one role inside a target account | You tailor a message to a job title | core |
| Lead | Contact who may buy but is not yet checked | You talk about top-of-funnel volume | core |
| Prospect | Lead that fits the ICP and is worth pursuing | You narrow leads to active targets | core |
| MQL (marketing qualified lead) | Lead that marketing judges ready for sales | You set the marketing-to-sales hand-off | core |
| SQL (sales qualified lead) | Lead that sales accepts as a real opportunity | You count pipeline that sales owns | core |
| Lead scoring | Points given to leads for fit and behavior | You rank leads for follow-up | core |
| Sequence (cadence) | Planned series of emails, calls, and social touches | You design multi-step outbound outreach | core |
| Trigger event | Change at an account that creates a reason to buy | You time outreach to a real reason | expert |
| Gatekeeper | Person who controls access to the decision maker | You plan how to reach executives | core |
| Meetings booked | Count of first meetings an SDR sets | You measure SDR output | core |
| Speed to lead (lead response time) | Time from lead arrival to first sales contact | You set inbound follow-up standards | expert |
| Territory | Accounts or regions that one rep owns | You assign accounts and prevent overlap | core |

### Qualification

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualification | Checking that a prospect can and will buy | You decide where reps spend time | core |
| BANT (budget, authority, need, timeline) | Four quick checks that a lead can buy | You need fast early-stage screening | core |
| MEDDIC (metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, identify pain, champion) | Six-part checklist for complex B2B deals | You qualify long, multi-stakeholder deals | expert |
| MEDDPICC | MEDDIC plus paper process and competition | Procurement and rivals add deal risk | expert |
| SPICED (situation, pain, impact, critical event, decision) | Discovery frame built for recurring-revenue sales | You sell subscriptions and plan for renewal | expert |
| Metrics (in MEDDIC) | Measured business gain the buyer expects | You build the value case | expert |
| Identify pain | Find the business problem that costs the buyer | You frame why they must act | core |
| Economic buyer (EB) | Person with final authority to approve the spend | You confirm who can sign | core |
| Decision criteria | Standards the buyer uses to compare options | You shape or answer the evaluation | expert |
| Decision process | Steps and people the buyer uses to decide | You map the path to a yes | expert |
| Paper process | Legal, security, and purchase steps to signature | You set a real close date | expert |
| Champion | Insider with power who sells for you internally | You need someone to push the deal | core |
| Critical event (compelling event) | Dated reason the buyer must act | You test urgency and close date | expert |
| Disqualify | Remove a deal that will not close | You clean pipeline and save rep time | core |

### Discovery and deal management

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery call | Meeting to learn the buyer's situation and pain | You plan an early meeting | core |
| Opportunity | Qualified deal tracked in the CRM with a value | You log and forecast a deal | core |
| Pipeline | All open opportunities, grouped by stage | You review deals in flight | core |
| Deal stage | Named step a deal has reached | You define the sales process | core |
| Exit criteria | Proof needed before a deal moves to the next stage | You stop reps from over-staging deals | expert |
| Demo | Product show tailored to the buyer's pain | You plan a mid-funnel meeting | core |
| POC (proof of concept) | Short trial that proves the product works for them | The buyer needs proof before buying | core |
| Mutual action plan (MAP) | Shared, dated list of steps to signature | You align buyer and seller on timing | expert |
| Buying committee | Group of people who decide the purchase together | You map stakeholders in a B2B deal | core |
| Multithreading | Building contacts with many people in one account | You reduce single-contact risk | expert |
| Business case | Document that shows the buyer's return on the spend | The buyer must justify spend internally | expert |
| Slippage | Deals whose close date moves to a later period | You audit forecast quality | expert |
| Closed-won / closed-lost | Final deal outcome: signed or lost | You report results | core |
| Win-loss analysis | Review of why deals were won or lost | You improve process, pitch, or product | expert |

### Negotiation and closing

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objection | Buyer concern that blocks the next step | You prepare responses | core |
| Objection handling | Method to answer a concern and confirm it is solved | You train reps or script calls | core |
| Trial close | Question that checks readiness without asking for the order | You test buyer commitment | core |
| Buying signal | Buyer words or acts that show intent to buy | You decide when to close | core |
| BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement) | Your best option if this deal fails | You set walk-away terms | expert |
| ZOPA (zone of possible agreement) | Range where both sides' acceptable terms overlap | You plan price moves | expert |
| Walk-away point (reservation price) | Worst terms you will accept | You set negotiation limits | expert |
| Anchoring | First number sets the reference for the talks | You choose your opening price | expert |
| Concession (give-get) | Term you give up in trade for a buyer commitment | You protect price late in a deal | core |
| Procurement | Buyer team that runs vendor terms and purchasing | You plan late-stage steps | core |
| Redlines | Contract edits that one party proposes | You negotiate legal terms | core |
| Deal desk | Team that approves nonstandard price and terms | A deal needs special approval | expert |
| Order form | Signed document that sets products, price, and term | You close a subscription deal | core |

### Sales ops and forecasting

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quota | Sales target for a rep or team in a period | You set goals and pay | core |
| Quota attainment | Percent of quota a rep closed | You judge rep performance | core |
| OTE (on-target earnings) | Base pay plus commission at full quota | You design or post a sales role | core |
| Comp plan (compensation plan) | Rules that turn closed deals into rep pay | You design incentives | core |
| Pipeline coverage | Open pipeline divided by remaining quota | You test if the target is reachable | core |
| Win rate | Won deals divided by all closed deals | You forecast and judge deal quality | core |
| Sales cycle length | Average days from opportunity open to close | You plan capacity and forecasts | core |
| Average deal size | Mean value of won deals | You model revenue | core |
| Sales velocity | Deals times win rate times size, divided by cycle days | You find which lever to pull | expert |
| Forecast category | Confidence bucket such as pipeline, best case, commit | You roll up a forecast | core |
| Commit | Deals the rep stakes the forecast on | You call the number for the period | core |
| Weighted pipeline | Each deal's value times its stage odds, summed | You build a simple forecast | expert |
| Bookings | Value of contracts signed in a period | You report sales output | core |
| ACV (annual contract value) | Yearly value of one contract | You compare deals of different lengths | core |
| ARR (annual recurring revenue) | Yearly value of all active subscriptions | You report subscription revenue | core |
| CRM hygiene | Keeping deal data in the CRM complete and current | You need reports you can trust | core |
| RevOps (revenue operations) | Team that runs process, data, and tools across go-to-market | You fix cross-team process | expert |
| Sales enablement | Training, content, and tools that help reps sell | You onboard or coach reps | core |

### Partnerships and channel

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Channel sales | Selling through third parties, not only a direct team | You extend reach without hiring reps | core |
| VAR (value-added reseller) | Partner that resells your product with its own services | You set up a resale model | core |
| Referral partner | Partner that sends leads in return for a fee | You want low-effort partner leads | core |
| Deal registration | Partner claims a deal for protection and better margin | You prevent partners competing for one deal | expert |
| Channel conflict | Partners or direct reps competing for the same deal | You write rules of engagement | expert |
| MDF (market development funds) | Vendor money for partner marketing | You fund partner demand generation | expert |
| Partner tiers | Partner levels with rising benefits and requirements | You structure a partner program | expert |
| PRM (partner relationship management) | System to recruit, train, and track partners | You run partner operations | expert |

### Retail and B2C selling

| Term | Plain meaning | Use it when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conversion rate (retail) | Share of visitors who buy | You judge store or floor selling | core |
| ATV (average transaction value) | Revenue divided by number of transactions | You track basket value | core |
| UPT (units per transaction) | Items sold divided by number of transactions | You measure add-on selling | core |
| Upsell | Move the buyer to a higher-priced option | You raise order value | core |
| Cross-sell (add-on sale) | Sell a related item with the main purchase | You raise basket size | core |
| Needs assessment | Questions that find what the shopper wants | You train sales associates | core |
| FAB (features, advantages, benefits) | Link product facts to what the buyer gains | You write a product pitch | core |
| Clienteling | Ongoing personal service for repeat high-value shoppers | You build repeat retail buyers | expert |

## Phrasebook

| Generic words | Trade words |
|---|---|
| find people to sell to | build a prospect list against the ICP (ideal customer profile) |
| the kind of company that buys from us | ICP and buyer personas |
| send a bunch of cold emails | run a multi-touch outbound sequence |
| follow up with people | add follow-up touches to the cadence |
| check if they are a real buyer | qualify with BANT or MEDDPICC |
| the person who can say yes | economic buyer (EB) |
| someone inside who likes us | champion |
| first sales meeting | discovery call |
| figure out what is wrong for them | identify pain and quantify impact |
| show them the product | run a tailored demo |
| let them try it first | scope a POC (proof of concept) with success criteria |
| plan to get the contract signed | mutual action plan (MAP) |
| legal and buying paperwork | paper process and procurement |
| deals we are working on | open pipeline by stage |
| how many deals we will close | forecast: commit and best case |
| do we have enough deals | pipeline coverage against remaining quota |
| how often we win | win rate (won over won plus lost) |
| how long deals take | sales cycle length |
| sales target | quota |
| how much reps earn | OTE (on-target earnings) and comp plan |
| deal keeps getting pushed | slippage |
| they said it is too expensive | price objection; handle with value, then give-get |
| our lowest acceptable deal | walk-away point and BATNA |
| sell more to the same customer | upsell, cross-sell, land and expand |
| sell through other companies | channel sales through resellers and referral partners |
| stop partners fighting over deals | deal registration and rules of engagement |
| why we lost the deal | win-loss analysis |
| bigger in-store purchases | raise ATV and UPT through add-on selling |

## Deliverable shapes

| Deliverable | Standard parts |
|---|---|
| ICP and persona sheet | firmographics, pains, triggers, buying committee roles, disqualifiers, sample accounts |
| Outbound sequence | target persona, trigger, touches by day and channel, copy per touch, personalization fields, exit condition, reply and meeting-booked targets |
| Discovery call guide | agenda, pre-call research, open questions by SPICED or MEDDPICC element, pain and impact probes, next step ask |
| Account plan | account overview, org and stakeholder map, whitespace, pains, competitors, opportunities, actions with owners and dates |
| Mutual action plan | shared goal, milestones, owners on both sides, dates, paper process steps, go-live date |
| Proposal | executive summary, buyer's situation and pain, proposed solution, business case and ROI, pricing and terms, timeline, next steps |
| Pipeline review / forecast call pack | commit and best case by rep, coverage, stage movement, slipped and at-risk deals, deal-level next steps |
| Sales playbook | ICP, personas, stages with exit criteria, qualification method, talk tracks, objection handling, tools and CRM rules |
| Competitive battlecard | competitor summary, where we win, where we lose, landmines to set, objection responses, proof points |
| Comp plan | role, OTE, base and variable split, quota, commission rates, accelerators, payout timing, clawback rules |
| Win-loss report | sample and method, win and loss reasons, competitor patterns, stage of loss, actions for sales, product, marketing |
| Partner program guide | partner types and tiers, requirements, benefits and margins, deal registration rules, MDF terms, enablement |

## Misused or dated terms

| Term | Problem | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| Lead (for any deal) | A lead is unqualified; a qualified deal with a value is an opportunity | lead, SQL, or opportunity, as the stage requires |
| Pipeline (as the forecast) | Pipeline is all open deals; the forecast is the subset you expect to close | forecast categories: commit, best case |
| Bookings (as revenue) | Bookings are signed contract value, not recognized revenue | bookings, ARR, or revenue, by meaning |
| Win rate (with open deals in the base) | Open deals have no outcome yet and distort the rate | won divided by won plus lost |
| ABC (always be closing) | Dated push-style advice that harms complex B2B deals | consultative selling with trial closes |
| Solution selling (as the current best practice) | Older model; current practice leads with insight and value | consultative, challenger, or value selling |
| MEDDIC as a sales process | It is a qualification checklist, not a full stage model | deal stages with exit criteria, qualified with MEDDIC |
| Discovery session, MAP, POC in buyer-facing copy | Internal jargon that confuses buyers | plain words, for example "a short call to learn your needs" |
